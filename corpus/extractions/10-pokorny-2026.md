# Extraction 10 — Pokorny (2026)

## Full citation (APA 7)
Pokorny, L. (2026). *Offensive cyber and information warfare strategies targeting
People's Republic of China military command, control, communications, computers,
intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (C4ISR) systems: A qualitative analysis*.
ICL Institute, Department of Military Science.
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19234589

## Research objective
To identify exploitable vulnerabilities in PRC military C4ISR architectures and develop
integrated offensive cyber and information warfare strategies across a 1–5 year
planning horizon, framed against a possible 2027 Taiwan timeline.

## Theoretical framework
Three named frameworks: **Lockheed Martin Cyber Kill Chain**, **Boyd's OODA Loop**, and
**Cyber Persistence Theory**. Also engages Libicki's taxonomy of cyber warfare and
MITRE ATT&CK.

## Methodology
Qualitative, multi-method **open-source intelligence (OSINT)** design analysing
**16 unclassified sources**: cyber threat intelligence reports, satellite system
databases, and PLA publications.

## Cases
16 PRC-attributed APT groups, including **Volt Typhoon**, APT41, APT1 (PLA Unit 61398).
Dedicated section: "Volt Typhoon and Critical Infrastructure Pre-Positioning" (p. 97).
Also analyses the **April 2024 dissolution of the Strategic Support Force** into the
Information Support Force (信息支援部队), Aerospace Force (航天部队), and Cyberspace
Force (网络空间部队).

## ★★ THE MOST IMPORTANT PASSAGE IN THE CORPUS ON THE BALANCE'S DIRECTION
> "Jon R. Lindsay (2013) has further enriched the theoretical debate by examining the
> relationship between cyber power and conventional military power. Lindsay argues that
> the technical complexity of cyber operations creates a paradox: **the more technically
> sophisticated the target, the more difficult it is to achieve a decisive cyber effect,
> because sophisticated defenders are better able to detect and mitigate intrusions.
> This 'offense-defense balance' in cyberspace, Lindsay contends, favors defense at the
> strategic level, even if individual tactical attacks may succeed.**"

**This is the only explicit statement in the corpus about which way the cyber
offence–defence balance tilts — and it says defence.**

It directly contradicts Butt & Ulina's (2026) unargued assertion that "defenders are
behind," and it converges with the counter-argument surfaced in extraction 08 (agentic
AI in the corpus is overwhelmingly defence-facing).

## ★ On forward operations — a gap Pokorny names
> "a significant gap in the public literature concerns the precise rules of engagement
> governing forward operations, **the potential for miscalculation**, and the legal
> implications of continuously operating within the sovereign networks of other states
> (Healey, 2019)."

This is the escalation/miscalculation problem, named as a literature gap, and it
converges with Sullivan's (2025) "fog of law" argument from a different direction.

## Organisational finding
USCYBERCOM's Cyber Mission Force comprises 133 teams across National Mission, Combat
Mission, and Protection Teams, "each with distinct roles in the offense-defense
spectrum" (citing Nakasone, 2019); Joint Cyber Warfighting Architecture (JCWA) as
integrating infrastructure.

### Contribution
1. **The Lindsay (2013) defence-favouring claim.** This is the corpus's only directional
   statement on the balance, and it runs *against* the offence-dominance intuition. It
   must be engaged in the literature review: if defence is favoured at the strategic
   level because sophisticated defenders detect and mitigate, then AI-enhanced detection
   *strengthens* an already-favoured defence, and the RQ's "how far" clause may have a
   counter-intuitive answer.
2. **A named pre-positioning section** — one of very few in the corpus.
3. **The 2024 SSF restructuring** into ISF/Aerospace Force/Cyberspace Force, with
   Chinese-character designations. Relevant to the geographic gap: this is the closest
   any corpus paper comes to PRC organisational detail.
4. **Miscalculation in forward operations** named as an open question.

### ★ Serious cautions — handle carefully
- **Self-published.** ICL Institute (New Jersey) with a Zenodo DOI; not a peer-reviewed
  press or journal. Single author, no visible review process.
- **Evidence base is thin for the claims made:** 16 unclassified sources supporting a
  full-length operational-planning monograph.
- **The text is internally inconsistent about what it is**, referring to itself as
  "this dissertation" and "this study" in different places.
- **Normative posture.** This is an *offensive targeting* document — it develops
  strategies for attacking PRC systems. That is a legitimate object of study, but it is
  a *primary source expressing a policy position*, not a neutral analysis. Using it as
  evidence about the offence–defence balance requires stating this.
- **Lindsay is cited, not reproduced.** The defence-favouring claim above is Pokorny's
  characterisation of Lindsay (2013). **Lindsay is not in the corpus.** Before this
  claim carries weight in the dissertation, the original must be obtained — the
  characterisation may not be faithful, and it is doing significant analytical work.

### Net assessment
**Valuable for one thing above all: it surfaces Lindsay's defence-favouring position,
which nothing else in the corpus states.** Treat the monograph itself as a secondary,
non-peer-reviewed source with a declared offensive posture; treat the Lindsay claim as
a lead to be verified against the original.
