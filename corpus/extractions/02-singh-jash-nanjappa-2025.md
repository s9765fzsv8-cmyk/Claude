# Extraction 02 — Singh, Jash & Nanjappa (2025)

## Full citation (APA 7)
Singh, N. K., Jash, A., & Nanjappa, Y. (2025). Navigating the nexus: Geopolitical,
international relations and technical dimensions of US-China cyber strategic
competition. *Cogent Social Sciences, 11*(1), 2499171.
https://doi.org/10.1080/23311886.2025.2499171

## Research objective
To examine how military and technological aspects of cyberspace shape US–China
geopolitical competition, and the implications for global cyber governance.

## Research question / hypothesis (as stated)
Evaluates the hypothesis that US–China cyberspace competition "is fuelled by three
factors: differing geopolitical factors, military considerations and technological
progress."

## Theoretical framework
**Offensive realism** and **techno-nationalism** (authors' explicit statement).

## Methodology
Review article using comparative and case-study approach; analysis of policy
documents, institutional frameworks, and cyber incidents.

## Cases
Operation Aurora, GhostNet, OPM breach, **Volt Typhoon**, Flax Typhoon, Salt Typhoon,
Silk Typhoon. Institutional comparison: USCYBERCOM vs China's ISF/CSF.

## Key concepts
Militarisation of cyberspace; cyber sovereignty vs multistakeholder governance;
techno-nationalism; standard-setting as contested terrain.

## Major arguments
1. Governance divergence (US multistakeholder vs Chinese cyber sovereignty) turns
   cyberspace into a battleground for standard-setting.
2. Militarisation is evident in military-intelligence competition, espionage, and
   weaponisation.
3. Volt Typhoon is periodised into an "advanced phase (2015–2024)" of competition.

## VERBATIM — Volt Typhoon as contested attribution (p. ~12)
> "in 2024, the direct targeting of Us critical infrastructure by the hacking group
> volt typhoon has infiltrated the Us telecommunications sectors. in response to the
> Us volt typhoon, the chinese national computer virus emergency response centre
> released a report further accusing the Us of operating the MarBle toolkit to erase
> the digital footprint."

The bibliography also cites the PRC Ministry of Foreign Affairs (2024) statement on
"US hyping up of 'Volt Typhoon'".

## Principal findings
US–China cyber competition operates across three interconnected dimensions:
geopolitical divergence in governance models, technical standard-setting, and
competing multilateral engagement.

## Limitations acknowledged
Review article; relies on open-source policy documents and secondary incident
reporting. (No formal limitations section identified in extracted text.)

**Assessment.** Useful for the *pre-positioning* half of the RQ, not the AI half.
Two specific contributions:

1. It places Volt Typhoon in a periodised sequence of US–China competition, which
   supports treating pre-positioning as a *phase* in strategic competition rather
   than a discrete incident.
2. It records the **PRC counter-narrative** — that Volt Typhoon is a US fabrication,
   with a reciprocal accusation about the MARBLE toolkit. This is analytically
   valuable: if pre-positioning is to function as a *signal*, the signal must be
   received and accepted as authentic. Contested attribution is precisely what
   degrades signalling. **This is the strongest empirical hook in the corpus so far
   for the RQ's second clause.**

**Caution.** The paper does not itself theorise pre-positioning as signalling. It
reports contestation; the signalling interpretation is my analytical synthesis and
must be labelled as such, not attributed to the authors.
