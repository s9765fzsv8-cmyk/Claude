## Research objective
To trace the PRC's refinement of grey-zone cyber tactics in Asia prior to their
application against Western critical infrastructure.

## Theoretical framework
**Grey zone** conflict theory — operations calibrated below use-of-force thresholds.
Draws on McCabe, "Strategic Warning in the Gray Zone" (2025); Harrington & McCabe
(2021); Franke, "War by Non-Military Means" (2024).

## Cases
**Volt Typhoon** (77 mentions — by far the densest treatment in the corpus), plus Flax,
Silk, Linen, Violet, Antique and Brass Typhoon. Russian operations against Ukrainian
power infrastructure 2015–16 as a calibration comparison.

## ★ THE GREY-ZONE CALIBRATION ARGUMENT
> "grey zone operations target strategically significant systems while **carefully
> calibrating effects to remain below use-of-force thresholds established in
> international law**. Rather than causing immediate, catastrophic damage that might
> trigger Article 5-type responses, these operations focus on persistent access,
> intelligence gathering, and **pre-positioning for potential future activation**. The
> 2015-2016 Russian operations against Ukrainian power infrastructure exemplify this
> calibration, causing temporary disruptions rather than permanent destruction."

## ★ The claimed strategic shift
> "PRC-linked operations have historically emphasized long-term strategic positioning,
> technical stealth, and comprehensive intelligence collection. Recent evidence,
> however, suggests **an evolution in PRC strategy toward pre-positioning disruptive
> capabilities** within critical infrastructure systems of geopolitical competitors,
> potentially for activation during periods of heightened tension or conflict."

## Scope claims
> "Compromises have now been publicly confirmed across **nearly every critical
> infrastructure sector in the United States**… also known to operate within the
> infrastructure of key allied and partner nations across both the West and Asia…
> found in **virtually every major global shipping canal**."

**Treat these claims with caution** — sweeping, sourced to advisories and secondary
reporting, in a non-reviewed preprint.

## Attribution
> "while technical forensics may eventually point to particular nation-states, **the
> deliberate obfuscation creates sufficient doubt to avoid immediate consequences**."

### Contribution
1. **Threshold calibration as deliberate design.** The claim that pre-positioning is
   *chosen* because it stays below Article 5 thresholds explains the response asymmetry
   documented in extractions 06 and 12: pre-positioning draws no sanctions **because it
   is engineered not to.** This is the corpus's best account of *why* the signalling
   channel underperforms.
2. **Obfuscation as consequence-avoidance** — attribution doubt as intended product, not
   technical accident. Complements Sullivan's (2025) technical/political attribution
   distinction.
3. **A geographic angle.** The Asia-first framing partially addresses the geographic gap.

### ★ How this challenges the thesis
Braccia's calibration argument **cuts against reading pre-positioning as a signal at
all.** If operations are designed to stay below response thresholds, avoiding legibility
is the point — the actor is not communicating resolve, it is acquiring options without
triggering reaction.

Pre-positioning would then be not a degraded signal but a **deliberate non-signal**, and
Guttieri's (2025) reading would be the target state inferring intent from an act designed
to avoid conveying it. Two rival hypotheses for the dissertation to test:

- **H1 (signal degradation):** pre-positioning once signalled intent; falling cost erodes
  that.
- **H2 (deliberate non-signal):** it was never intended to signal; sub-threshold
  calibration is the design goal.

The three-case evidence — non-use over 7+ years, no sanctions, effects calibrated below
Article 5 — is arguably **more consistent with H2.** This is a significant challenge to
the working thesis and is recorded as such.
