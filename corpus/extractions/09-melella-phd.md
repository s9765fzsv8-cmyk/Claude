## Research objective
To analyse the role of **coordination** in offensive and defensive cyber operations
across China, Russia and NATO, and its impact on national and international security.

## Research questions (as stated)
"Conceptualizing the lack of coordination in cyberspace and understanding its
implications for the effectiveness of cyber operations."

## Theoretical framework
Offence–defence balance in cyberspace; coercion theory (engages Borghard & Lonergan,
"The Logic of Coercion in Cyberspace," 2017); Valeriano, Jensen & Maness; asymmetry
in computer network operations (Wiener).

## Methodology
Literature review + theoretical exploration + empirical case studies + dataset
development.

## Cases
Sino-Russian coordination during the Ukraine war; internal dynamics of Russian
intelligence agencies (GRU, SVR, FSB APT groups); **NATO Locked Shields exercise**
(Ch. VI — ML-based IDS dataset development).

## ★★★ STRUCTURE — the corpus's only sustained offence–defence treatment

- 4.1 Cyber Posturing and the Offense-Defense Dynamics in Cybersecurity Literature
- 4.2 Balancing Offense and Defense in Cyber Operations: A Review of the Evolving
  Cybersecurity Landscape
- 4.3 Exploring Gaps in Cybersecurity: Beyond Offense-Defense Balance

Chapter II, 1.2: "Redefining Cyber Warfare Strategies: **Beyond Offense and Defense in
the Digital Age**"

## ★★★ THE CRITICAL PASSAGE — MELELLA NAMES THIS RESEARCH QUESTION AS A GAP
> "**The advancement of technologies, particularly artificial intelligence, is changing
> the landscape of cyberspace. Studying how these emerging technologies affect the
> offensive-defensive balance could reveal new dynamics and challenges.**"

> "An under-explored area is **how actors perceive the offensive-defensive balance and
> how these perceptions influence their policy and strategy decisions.** Understanding
> these perceptions could offer new insights into the dynamics of cyberspace."

## AI in the dissertation
Chapter VI "examines the potential of artificial intelligence in solving coordination
problems in cyber operations… the NATO Locked Shields exercise, in which innovative
datasets were developed for an intrusion detection system (IDS) based on machine
learning techniques."

Also notes, via Wiener, "the integration of artificial intelligence machine learning and
autonomous systems" as a direction requiring continued study.

### ★ CORRECTION TO THE EARLIER FINDING — required

Extractions 01–08 supported the claim that **no corpus paper connects AI autonomy to
the offence–defence balance** ("eight for eight"). **That claim as stated is now too
strong and must be qualified.**

Melella connects them explicitly. He devotes a full chapter section to the
offence–defence balance in cyberspace, uses the term 21 times, engages AI across a
dedicated chapter, and directly proposes their intersection as a research direction.

**However, the gap survives in a more precise form.** Melella *names* the intersection
as future research; he does not *investigate* it. His own dependent variable is
**coordination**, not the balance, and his AI chapter concerns ML for intrusion
detection in an exercise setting — defensive tooling, not strategic consequence. The
offence–defence material is literature review; the AI material is dataset engineering;
the two are not joined analytically anywhere in the dissertation.

**Restated finding:** *The intersection of AI autonomy and the offence–defence balance
has been identified as a research gap within the corpus, by one author, and remains
unfilled by any corpus paper — including his.*

This is a **weaker claim about novelty but a stronger claim about warrant.**

### ★ Why this is good news for the dissertation
1. **Independent validation.** A doctoral researcher in security studies, working the
   same terrain, arrived at the same gap independently. This is the single best
   available answer to a reviewer asking "why does this question matter?"
2. **A citable gap statement.** "Studying how these emerging technologies affect the
   offensive-defensive balance could reveal new dynamics and challenges" can be quoted
   directly in the Research Gap section as prior recognition.
3. **A second gap that fits the argument exactly.** Melella's remark that "how actors
   *perceive* the offensive-defensive balance" is under-explored maps precisely onto
   this paper's signalling framing — perception and interpretation, not capability
   ratios, are the analytical object.
4. **The corpus's only offence–defence literature review.** §4.1–4.3 partially
   compensates for the absence of Slayton (2017), and its bibliography is a route to
   the canonical works.
