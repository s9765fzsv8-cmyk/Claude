# Extraction 06 — Baram (2026) ★★ EMPIRICAL ANCHOR

## Full citation (APA 7)
Baram, G. (2026). Re-ordering accountability: The significance of joint public
attribution in a fragmented cyberspace. *Contemporary Security Policy*. Advance online
publication. https://doi.org/10.1080/13523260.2026.2662220

Affiliations: Bar-Ilan University; Center for Long-Term Cybersecurity, UC Berkeley.

**Venue note.** *Contemporary Security Policy* is a ranked, peer-reviewed security
studies journal — **the highest-quality venue in the corpus alongside Maschmeyer's
*EJIR*.** This paper carries citation weight the rest of the corpus does not.

## Research objective
To explain variation in "jointness" when two or more states publicly attribute the
same cyber operation, and to map the practice empirically.

## Research question (as stated)
Why do governments choose to act together, when do they choose one form of joint
attribution over another, and what is each form designed to achieve?

## Theoretical framework
Joint public attribution located "at the intersection of cyber norms, deterrence
signaling, and alliance politics." Builds on Rupp and Paulus (2023) on attribution and
norm formation; engages Borghard & Lonergan (2019), Kreps & Schneider (2019),
Egloff (2020), Rid & Buchanan (2015).

## Methodology ★ (model for the dissertation)
Typology construction + empirical coding of **20 joint public attribution incidents,
2015–2025**, drawn from the **European Repository of Cyber Incidents (EuRepoC;
Zettl-Schabath et al., 2025)**.

## Typology — four modes of jointness
1. Synchronized national statements (5 cases)
2. Alliance or regional endorsement (7 cases)
3. Joint technical advisories (4 cases)
4. Joint action packages — sanctions, indictments, expulsions (4 cases)

## ★★ THE CRITICAL FINDING FOR THE RQ
> "**Volt Typhoon (May 2023) includes a shared advisory with pooled IoCs and defensive
> guidance, co-authored by US and Five Eyes agencies. No alliance-led endorsement or
> sanctions package followed. I code it as Type 3.**"

**The paradigmatic pre-positioning case drew the weakest available form of collective
response.** No sanctions. No alliance endorsement. A technical advisory.

## ★★ AND THE MECHANISM — who the signal is addressed to
> "Joint technical advisories are most plausible when governments prioritize defense
> outcomes… This type **clusters in China-related cases focused on stealthy access and
> pre-positioning against critical infrastructure** and telecommunications… **The
> primary audience here is network defenders, not only state elites.**"

## Distribution of cases
Russia 11 (disruptive campaigns); **China 7 — "primarily involving long-term espionage
and pre-positioning within critical infrastructure and telecommunications"**; North
Korea 1 (WannaCry); Iran 1 (Albania wiper).

## Temporal shift
> "Beginning in 2023, focus shifted toward stealthy access operations against critical
> infrastructure. Volt Typhoon and its 2024 update detailed 'living off the land'
> techniques aimed at US water, energy, and communications systems."

## Timing and political salience
> "Joint public attributions occurred during politically sensitive moments, such as the
> Tokyo Olympics, European elections, and the COVID-19 pandemic. These patterns may
> suggest that governments time public attributions based not only on technical
> evidence but also…" [on political considerations].

## Limitations acknowledged ★ (exemplary — model this)
> "this dataset primarily reflects Western and allied attribution practices, as these
> actors consistently publish the most comprehensive documentation. Joint attribution
> efforts in the Global South, BRICS or other regional forums may be underreported. The
> findings, therefore, map Western-led joint public attributions rather than global
> practices (on bias in cyber research see Vićić, Baram, & Gartzke, 2026)."

## Principal findings
Alliance endorsement is the most frequent mode. Growth in joint technical advisories
and broader coalitions indicates expanding multilateral capacity. The target of
coordinated response shifted from Russian disruption (pre-2023) to Chinese
espionage and pre-positioning (2023 onward).

## RELEVANCE TO RQ — critical assessment

**Term counts (verified):** `attribut*` 211 | `signal*` 28 | `deterren*` 20 |
`norm` 58 | `escalat*` 14 | `Volt Typhoon` 7 | `pre-position*` 3 |
`artificial intelligence` **0** | `autonom*` **0** | `agentic` **0** |
`offense-defense` **0**

**Six for six. No corpus paper connects AI autonomy to strategy.** The gap is now
documented across two ranked journals (*EJIR*, *CSP*), the *Cyber Defense Review*
(×2), and two lower-tier venues.

### ★★ This finding forces a revision of the working thesis

The draft argued that AI autonomy will *erode* pre-positioning's signalling function by
lowering persistence cost. Baram's data suggests **that function is already weak — and
supplies the reason why.**

Volt Typhoon received a Type 3 response: a technical advisory whose "primary audience
is network defenders, not only state elites." Sanctions and alliance endorsement — the
instruments that would mark a state treating an act as *coercive* — did not follow.
This is not merely non-response. It is **response routed to the wrong addressee for
signalling purposes.** A coercive signal must reach political decision-makers to
function. Pre-positioning is being routed to system administrators.

This converges independently with Sullivan (2025), who argues such operations "lack
sufficient political salience to activate political institutions to punish the
perpetrator," and with Singh et al. (2025), who record the PRC's flat denial.

**Three documented failure modes for pre-positioning-as-signal:**
1. **Non-reception** — insufficient political salience (Sullivan, 2025)
2. **Denial** — the putative sender disowns the signal (Singh et al., 2025)
3. **Misrouting** — response is technical, addressed to defenders not elites
   (Baram, 2026, with data)

### Consequence for the argument
The thesis should be restated. Not "AI will break the signalling function," but:
**pre-positioning already functions poorly as a signal, and the corpus shows why;
autonomy operates on a mechanism that is already failing.** This is a stronger and
better-evidenced claim, and it inverts Guttieri's (2025) confidence that
pre-positioning "likely reflects preparation for conflict" — Guttieri establishes what
pre-positioning *means*; Baram shows states do not *act* as though it means that.

**That tension — between interpretation and response — is the paper's empirical core.**

### Methodological model
EuRepoC is a usable, citable dataset for the dissertation, and Baram's coding approach
(typology → case coding → pattern analysis) is directly transferable to coding
pre-positioning incidents on Guttieri's intelligence-yield/leverage dimensions.
Her limitations paragraph is a model of the Western-bias declaration the dissertation
will also owe.
