# Extraction 11 — Codreanu (2025) ★★ PRE-POSITIONING CASE EVIDENCE

## Full citation (APA 7)
Codreanu, C. (2025). *Typhoons, bears, and pandas: Latest major cyber espionage
campaigns* (Policy Paper No. 43). Romanian Diplomatic Institute.
(English translation of an article originally published in Romanian, July 2025.)

## Research objective
To analyse contemporary interstate cyber espionage, distinguishing Chinese campaigns
(Volt Typhoon, Salt Typhoon) from Russian ones, and to assess commercial spyware.

## Theoretical framework
None explicit. Policy analysis. Engages Maschmeyer & Dunn Cavelty (2022) and
**Lonergan & Poznansky (2025)**.

## Methodology
Open-source policy analysis. Explicit source declaration: "This publication draws
exclusively on open-source materials."

## Cases
**Volt Typhoon**, Salt Typhoon, SolarWinds, Cozy Bear/APT29, Russian campaigns against
Ukraine, Pegasus spyware; 2018 Russian infiltration of US critical infrastructure.

## ★★ THE ESPIONAGE / PRE-POSITIONING DISTINCTION, STATED DIRECTLY
> "Washington emphasized that **the method of operation and chosen targets did not fit
> the pattern of a traditional cyber espionage campaign** (Davidson 2024). US
> authorities warned that the malware found within US infrastructure appeared to be
> prepared for use in the event that Washington intervened militarily to support Taiwan
> amid a potential escalation (Sanger & Landler 2024). Unlike other Chinese operations,
> the United States and its allies argued that **the goal was not the extraction of data
> for conventional espionage but rather the pre-positioning in key points of critical
> infrastructure to prepare for possible future acts of sabotage** (Davidson 2024)."

This independently reproduces Guttieri's (2025) decision rule — low intelligence yield
plus high leverage implies preparation, not collection — from a different institutional
vantage (Romanian Diplomatic Institute) and different sources. **Two independent
statements of the same inferential criterion strengthen it considerably.**

## ★★★ THE MOST IMPORTANT FINDING: A NEGATIVE CASE
> "In 2018, Washington accused Russia of infiltrating multiple sectors of US critical
> infrastructure, including nuclear facilities, energy, aviation, and even water supply
> networks (Lonergan & Poznansky 2025). **So far, however, Russia has not exploited
> these footholds to launch direct cyberattacks on the US** – though the risks have grown
> significantly since the war of aggression against Ukraine (Lonergan & Poznansky 2025)."

**This is the corpus's only case of pre-positioning followed by non-use, and it has run
for seven-plus years.**

Its analytical value is high:
- It supplies **variation on the dependent variable**, which extraction 06 identified as
  absent from the corpus and which the methodology in §8 requires.
- It **directly qualifies the Nakasone/Guttieri inference.** If pre-positioning "likely
  reflects preparation for conflict," the Russian case shows preparation can persist for
  years without conflict. Pre-positioning is then better read as **option-generation**
  than as commitment — consistent with Guttieri's own "portfolio of future options"
  language, but cutting against the warning-indicator reading her rule invites.
- It creates a **paired comparison**: Russia 2018 (pre-positioned, not used) versus
  China 2023 (pre-positioned, not used *to date*), with different attributional and
  political responses.

## ★ Third independent record of PRC denial
> "Lin Jian, the spokesperson for the Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs, rejected the
> allegations, dismissing them as **disinformation** (Sanger & Landler 2024)."

With Singh et al. (2025) on the MARBLE counter-accusation and the PRC MFA "hyping up"
statement, the corpus now documents PRC denial from three independent sources.

## Attribution practice
May 2023 joint statement by NSA, CISA, DOJ with Canada, UK, Australia — consistent with
Baram's (2026) Type 3 coding. May 2025: CISA joint statement on Russian campaigns
co-signed by eleven states (Australia, Canada, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, France,
Germany, Netherlands, Poland, UK).

## Policy recommendations
Public attribution of campaigns; sanctions; strengthened cybersecurity; regulation of
commercial spyware export and use.

### Assessment
**The corpus's richest single source on Volt Typhoon as pre-positioning** (18 mentions,
against Guttieri's 13), and the only source supplying a negative case.

Its central contribution to the argument is the Russian 2018 comparison. The working
thesis holds that pre-positioning's signalling function is degrading; the Russian case
suggests it may never have functioned as a *commitment* signal at all — persistent
access has been maintained for seven years without use or escalation. This supports
reframing pre-positioning as **option-generation under uncertainty** rather than as
warning, which in turn changes what AI autonomy would do to it: autonomy would scale
option-generation, not accelerate attack.

### Cautions
- **Not peer-reviewed.** An institute policy paper, and a translation from Romanian.
- **Wholly reliant on journalism** for its empirical claims — Sanger, Davidson,
  Greenberg & Newman, Sanger & Landler. Cite the underlying reporting where possible.
- The Lonergan & Poznansky (2025) claim about Russian non-use is second-hand here and
  **should be verified in the original**, since it now carries substantial analytical
