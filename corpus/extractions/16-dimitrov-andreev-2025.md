## ★★ THE EXPLICIT H1 CLAIM — pre-positioning AS deterrence and coercion
> "The activities of Volt Typhoon underscore China's increasing reliance on the
> **prepositioning of cyber assets in U.S. critical infrastructure as a means of
> deterrence and coercion**. Unlike conventional cyberespionage campaigns, which
> primarily seek to obtain intelligence for diplomatic, military, or economic purposes,
> Volt Typhoon operates with the clear goal of infiltrating vital national infrastructure
> while remaining undetected for long periods."

> "not merely intelligence gathering missions, but **pre-emptive measures designed to
> embed China's cyber capabilities into critical infrastructure, ensuring its influence
> in times of crisis**."

**This is the corpus's clearest statement of H1** — that pre-positioning functions as a
coercive instrument, i.e. a signal. It stands in direct opposition to Braccia's (2025)
H2 (sub-threshold calibration designed to *avoid* legibility).

**The corpus therefore contains explicit advocates for both hypotheses.** This is a live
scholarly disagreement, not a gap the dissertation invents — a considerably stronger
position from which to write.

## ★ The Guam case
> "its infiltration of U.S. military networks in Guam, a critical base for Indo-Pacific
> operations. On May 24, 2023, Microsoft disclosed evidence of Volt Typhoon malware
> found within the network infrastructure linked to U.S. Department of Defence operations
> in Guam… Guam serves as a major hub for U.S. military readiness in the Indo-Pacific,
> playing a vital role in **power projection, logistics and rapid response operations,
> particularly concerning Taiwan**. **While forensic investigations did not reveal
> immediate sabotage**, the intrusions pointed to intelligence gathering and strategic
> mapping of critical communication networks."

**Note the internal tension.** The authors assert pre-positioning is coercive, yet
concede the Guam forensics "did not reveal immediate sabotage" and pointed to
"intelligence gathering and strategic mapping." Their own evidence is consistent with the
espionage reading they reject. **This is an exploitable weakness in the H1 case and
should be noted in the literature review.**

## ★ Target selection as evidence of intent
Guam matters because it is the Indo-Pacific power-projection hub for a Taiwan
contingency. This supplies a **target-selection criterion** complementary to Guttieri's
(2025) intelligence-yield/leverage rule: pre-positioning in nodes whose value is
*specific to a named contingency* is stronger evidence of intent than pre-positioning in
generic infrastructure.
