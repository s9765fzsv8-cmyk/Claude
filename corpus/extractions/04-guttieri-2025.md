# Extraction 04 — Guttieri (2025) ★ HIGHEST VALUE SO FAR

## Full citation (APA 7)
Guttieri, K. (2025). Fighting through disruption: Reframing cyber resilience for
power projection and strategic credibility. *The Cyber Defense Review, 10*(1), 93–114.
https://doi.org/10.55682/cdr/egvf-mkys

Author affiliation: Army Cyber Institute, West Point.

## Research objective
To argue for a dynamic conception of cyber resilience — "not merely withstanding
disruption, but fighting through it, adapting in contact, and sustaining initiative
in a contested environment."

## Theoretical framework
- **Woods' layered resilience model**: robustness / graceful extensibility /
  sustained adaptability.
- Engages **Cyber Persistence Theory** (Fischerkeller, Goldman & Harknett),
  **Buchanan's cybersecurity dilemma**, **Harknett & Smeets' cyber campaigning**,
  and **Cunningham (2022)** on cyber as a stand-in for nuclear escalation.

## Methodology
Conceptual argument with illustrative case studies. Also uses Google Books NGram
data on term frequency (author concedes this is "more illustrative than" probative).

## Cases
**Volt Typhoon** (13 mentions); Ukraine's cyber defence; Jack Voltaic exercises.

## ★ CORE PASSAGE FOR THE RQ — pre-positioning as latent power
> "The discovery of threat actors pre-positioned in CI—sometimes for several years—is
> alarming because their presence is intentionally subtle. As in the case of Volt
> Typhoon, attackers use native system utilities and cover their tracks in a technique
> known as Living Off the Land (LOTL). This combination of technical competence and
> strategic patience enables them to evade detection and **maintain a portfolio of
> future options**, allowing them to choose from escalating the attack, disrupting
> services, conducting surveillance, or withdrawing—all sequenced and timed to achieve
> the desired effect. Over time, intruders accumulate **a form of latent power on the
> network**."

## ★★ CORE PASSAGE — when pre-positioning IS a signal (direct answer to RQ clause 2)
> "General Paul Nakasone dismissed the notion that Volt Typhoon was merely collecting
> intelligence, saying, 'There is absolutely no intelligence to be gathered by putting
> malicious code in [critical infrastructure] networks…' Cyber analyst James Lewis
> reinforced this view, asking, 'Why would [they] do reconnaissance if [they] were not
> thinking of an attack?' **Taken together, their insights offer a strong interpretive
> signal: when persistent access offers low intelligence gain but high leverage, it
> likely reflects preparation for conflict.**"

**This supplies a decision rule.** Pre-positioning reads as a strategic signal — not
espionage — when the *intelligence yield is low relative to the operational leverage
gained*. That is a usable analytical criterion for the dissertation.

## ★ CORE PASSAGE — the destabiliser question
> "Fiona Cunningham (2022) […] suggests that China deliberately employs cyber,
> counter-space, and precision systems as stand-ins for nuclear escalation, leveraging
> them for **signaling and coercion**. […] **Rather than deterring escalation, China's
> use of cyber operations appears designed to shape conditions for conflict.**"

> "the operational interdependence and entanglement among cyber, space, and nuclear
> systems complicate the idea of a 'clean' strategic substitute. While some suggest
> that this complexity adds to the coercive power of these tools by introducing
> ambiguity and the risk of escalation (Cunningham 2022), **it also undermines their
> reliability as controlled instruments of deterrence or coercion**. […] even a limited
> cyberattack could inadvertently disrupt critical infrastructure and escalate tensions
> far beyond its intended scope."

## ★ Cybersecurity dilemma
Guttieri invokes Buchanan's "cybersecurity dilemma"—"an extension of the traditional
security dilemma in which a state's efforts to secure itself provoke escalation from
others." This is the classic offence–defence mechanism, arriving under a different name.

## Deterrence critique
> "The prevailing U.S. focus on deterrence by retaliation, including offensive cyber
> responses, underestimates challenges of attribution, escalation management, and
> cross-domain friction. Deterrence by punishment is difficult to execute when
> adversaries can exploit ambiguity. In contrast, preventing adversaries from
> achieving their goals through resilience remains insufficiently emphasized."

I.e. an argument for **deterrence by denial** over deterrence by punishment.

## Principal findings
Prevailing resilience definitions (NIST) are IT-centric, static, rebound-focused, and
insufficient for conflict conditions. Resilience is "not a state, but a practice and
a core operational capability." Institutional fragmentation at the civil-military seam
undermines integrated defence.

## Limitations acknowledged
Author concedes the NGram evidence is "more illustrative than" conclusive. Standard
US Army disclaimer that views are not official policy.

## RELEVANCE TO RQ — critical assessment

**Term counts (verified):** `Volt Typhoon` 13 | `resilien*` 152 | `escalat*` 11 |
`deterren*` 7 | `signal` 3 | `pre-position`/`prepositioning` 3 |
`offense-defense` **0** | `artificial intelligence` **0** | `autonom*` **0** |
`agentic` **0**

**Assessment. This is the anchor paper for the second half of the RQ.** It is the
only paper in the corpus so far that treats pre-positioning as a *strategic* problem
rather than a technical one, and it supplies:
- a mechanism (latent power / portfolio of future options),
- a decision rule for reading pre-positioning as signal (low intelligence yield +
  high leverage ⇒ preparation for conflict),
- an explicit destabilisation argument (entanglement undermines controllability),
- a deterrence-by-denial policy position,
- and named theoretical scaffolding (Cyber Persistence Theory, cybersecurity
  dilemma, cyber campaigning, Cunningham on cyber-as-nuclear-substitute).

**The decisive limitation: zero engagement with AI.** Guttieri answers "when is
pre-positioning a signal?" without any reference to autonomy. The RQ's first clause
— *pace* of AI autonomy — is untouched.

**Analytical opportunity (my synthesis, not the author's).** Guttieri's decision rule
is stated in terms of *human* strategic patience: "technical competence and strategic
patience." If agentic autonomy lowers the cost of maintaining persistent access, the
"strategic patience" premise weakens — pre-positioning at scale would no longer signal
deliberate high-level intent, because it would no longer be expensive. **That is
precisely the unresolved question the RQ names, and Guttieri supplies the baseline
against which it can be posed.** This inference is mine and must be labelled as such.
