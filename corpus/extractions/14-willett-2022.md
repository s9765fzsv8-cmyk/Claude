# Extraction 14 — Willett (2022) ★★★ BEST SOURCE FOR THE OFFENCE–DEFENCE CLAUSE

## Full citation (APA 7)
Willett, M. (2022). The cyber dimension of the Russia–Ukraine war. *Survival, 64*(5),
7–26. https://doi.org/10.1080/00396338.2022.2126193

**Author.** Marcus Willett, IISS Senior Adviser for Cyber; previously GCHQ, where he
"helped design UK cyber strategy, and initiated and led several national cyber
programmes." Article adapted from the Adelphi book *On Offensive Cyber: The Responsible
Use of Cyber Power*.

**Venue.** *Survival* (IISS) — **the highest-ranking strategic studies venue in the
corpus.** 21,928 article views; 48 citing articles.

## Research objective
To assess the character and significance of the cyber dimension of the 2022
Russia–Ukraine war, against expectations of "full-on, full-scale cyberwar."

## Theoretical framework
Not formalised. Engages Gerasimov's 2013 thesis on blurred war/peace boundaries and
non-military means; Article 5 thresholds; the "responsible use of cyber power."

## Methodology
Expert analytic assessment drawing on Microsoft, Cisco, Google, ESET reporting;
Ukrainian authorities; UK/US official statements.

## ★★★ THE DIRECTIONAL FINDING — defence dominated, empirically
> "it has also included a sustained Russian campaign to hack into and disrupt Ukraine's
> critical national infrastructure, resulting in **intense sparring between offence and
> defence, but with defence dominating most of the time**, given its access to good
> intelligence and top-class cyber-security expertise."

**This is the corpus's only empirically grounded statement on the offence–defence
balance in an actual war, and it finds for defence.** It corroborates Lindsay (via
Pokorny, 2026) and converges with the defence-facing character of corpus agentic AI
(Yigit et al., 2025).

## ★★★ AND THE BALANCE IS CONDITIONAL, NOT FIXED
> "a cyber conflict between Russia and a state with weaker cyber security than Ukraine,
> or one between NATO and Russia (or China), would perhaps see **a different balance
> between offence and defence**."

**Theoretically decisive.** The balance is not a property of the technology but of the
*dyad* — specifically of defender capability. This reframes the RQ: AI autonomy does not
shift "the" balance; it shifts **whichever side can better absorb it**, and Willett's
evidence is that in Ukraine the absorbing side was the defender, assisted by US/UK
agencies and by Microsoft, Google and Cisco.

## ★★ PRE-POSITIONING — a third case type
> "the Russians had been conducting reconnaissance of, and **pre-positioning cyber
> capabilities on**, certain Ukrainian energy and communications networks from at least
> March 2021."

> "Moscow has also envisaged the deliberate use of such operations beyond the conflict
> zone, and **has been pre-positioning relevant capabilities, the only uncertainty being
> the conditions under which Russia might resort to attempting to use them.**"

Crucially, Ukraine supplies the corpus's **only case of pre-positioning followed by
attempted use** — and the attempt failed:

> "an intrusion into one of Ukraine's largest energy facilities in February, set to
> deliver a disruptive effect on 8 April… **Ukrainian cyber security neutralised it.**
> The Ukrainians reported that while the malware (called 'Industroyer2') had penetrated
> the electricity grid's management system, and would have shut off power for two
> million people had the attack succeeded, **no power outages in fact occurred.**"

Similarly, 'Pipedream' malware targeting LNG safety controllers was "detected and
disrupted" before use; the FBI disabled a Russian router botnet "before it could be
used for a destructive attack."

### The case typology is now complete
| Case | Pre-positioned | Used | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Russia → US CI (2018– ) | ✓ | ✗ | 7+ yrs non-use (Codreanu, 2025) |
| Volt Typhoon (2023– ) | ✓ | ✗ | Non-use to date; Type 3 response (Baram, 2026) |
| **Russia → Ukraine (2021–22)** | ✓ | **✓** | **Attempt defeated by defence** (Willett, 2022) |

**Three pre-positioning cases; one attempted use; zero successful strategic effects.**

## ★ Operations as signals
> "it is conceivable that the war might have given the US and UK an opportunity to
> **signal their cyber capabilities and capacity to the Russians in new ways to
> deterrent effect**."

On the 2015–16 grid attacks: "both operations **signalled a clear Russian intent and
capability** to impede Ukraine's ability to function as a state." Note that here the
signal is carried by *use*, not by *presence* — a distinction the dissertation should
draw explicitly.

## ★ Escalation
- Biden: a cyber breach would be the most likely cause of the US being "in a real
  shooting war" with a major power.
- NATO assesses Article 5 thresholds case-by-case and "could consider an **accumulation
  of minor attacks** as crossing that threshold."
- Cyber vigilantism (~300,000 participants by May 2022) "increased the risk of spillover…
  and the possibility of **incorrect attribution leading to retaliation and escalation**."
- Willett notes Russia likely learned from Estonia 2007 "what sorts of cyber attacks on
  a NATO state the Alliance would **not** consider close to its threshold" — threshold
  discovery through probing.

## Principal findings
Russian cyber operations were "less effective than expected" (Fleming, GCHQ). Key
Russian weaknesses: inability to coordinate cyber with kinetic effects, poor domestic
cyber security, and a "dearth of the sorts of capabilities needed to surgically impair
military units, weapons systems, and command and control." Ukrainian resilience was
decisive, aided by US/UK agencies and Western private-sector capacity.

## Limitations acknowledged
Explicitly bounded: the war "has not yet involved both sides using top-end offensive
cyber capabilities against each other (and is perhaps therefore not full-on, full-scale
cyber war)." Assessment current "as of August 2022."

## RELEVANCE TO RQ — critical assessment

**AI engagement: none.** No discussion of artificial intelligence, machine learning,
autonomy, or agentic systems anywhere in the article. **Fourteen extractions; still only
Melella connects AI to the balance, and only as a proposed direction.**

### Why this is the most important extraction for clause 1
1. It supplies the **only wartime empirical judgement** on the offence–defence balance
   in the corpus, and it favours defence.
2. It establishes the balance as **dyad-conditional**, not technology-determined —
   which reframes the RQ from "does AI favour offence or defence?" to "**which side is
   better positioned to absorb autonomy, and under what conditions?**"
3. It supplies the **third case type**, completing a design with genuine variation.
4. It documents **threshold discovery by probing** (Estonia 2007) — a signalling
   mechanism distinct from pre-positioning, and a possible control case.

### Caution
Written in 2022 and explicitly time-bounded; the assessment predates both the Volt
Typhoon attribution and the agentic-AI wave. Cite for the offence–defence judgement and
the Ukraine case, not for current capability estimates.
